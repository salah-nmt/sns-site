01

Order to cash, without the gaps

2026 · ongoing

NMT Group · Antwerp · multi-million euro installer

Quotes, payments, scheduling and invoicing lived in tools that did not talk to each other. Orders stalled in the gaps between them, and the first signal was usually a customer calling to ask what happened.

What I built

  • A quote the customer configures themselves online, with payment at the moment of decision instead of two weeks later
  • Capacity-aware scheduling, including jobs that run across several days
  • 13 automated messages across 11 order stages, so nobody chases status by hand
  • Reconciliation into the accounting package and the bank, so invoices and payments match without manual work

The control layer

  • A watchdog that checks every five minutes whether any order has stalled, and flags it before the customer notices
  • Six monitored background jobs with their own status page and heartbeat
  • Demand planning aligned with sales and finance: campaign volume is planned against installer capacity, so we sell what we can deliver
  • 43 data models and 97 API routes, written and maintained by me

02

Four markets, one position on stock

2025 · ongoing

Relivize · Calmera · own D2C brands · NL, BE, FR, US

Four countries with different suppliers, different payment habits and different shipping economics, all drawing on one inventory position. Every reorder is my own capital, so the forecast has to be right rather than defensible.

Operations

  • Supplier selection, negotiation and reorder decisions across all four markets
  • Pricing set per market against local shipping cost and payment fees
  • Payment methods chosen per country: Bancontact in Belgium, iDEAL in the Netherlands, cards and wallets in the US

Visibility

  • Per-market reporting on margin, stock and repeat rate, built so I never wait on a platform export
  • Order and fulfilment data pulled through the store APIs into one place I can query
  • Landed cost tracked per market, so a market that looks profitable on revenue can be checked on contribution
03

Stock that expires

2021 – 2023

Borz Nutrition · Antwerp · sports nutrition, B2B to gyms

A physical product with a shelf life, sold to gyms across Belgium that reorder on a rhythm. This is where the forecasting instinct came from, and it cost me real money to build it.

What I owned

  • Purchasing, stock levels, reorder cycles and B2B distribution
  • Supplier relationships and the terms behind them
  • Shelf life management, so slow-moving lines were pushed before they became write-offs

The lesson that stuck

  • Over-ordering ties up cash a young brand does not have
  • Under-ordering hands the account to whoever can deliver on Monday
  • The gap between those two is the entire job, and it is a forecasting problem long before it is a logistics one
